## A. 3D anatomy exercise demo (Workit, Fitonomy x2)
- A mannequin or anatomy body does the exercise with the target muscle highlighted in red. The camera sits in a clean 3D gym. 15 to 65 s.
- Hooks: "Want bigger/stronger X?", "X vs Y" (stance, grip, variation), "Same machine, different muscles".
- **Save-driven.** Saves run at 2 to 6% of views, shares are low. Steady, with a high floor.
- Workit goes 0 to 324k in about 7 months at roughly 1 to 2 posts a day. These are app accounts, and the bio link goes to download or a quiz.
- EARND fit: high. "Same lift, different setup" and how to load and progress a lift tie to tracking. The niche is crowded, though.

## B. Sketch-style animated how-to (@h.a.m.e.e.d.k.h.a.n1)
- White line-drawn anatomy on black, a title bar at the top, 90 to 160 s. Parts 1/2/3 split one topic.
- Two megahits ("How To Force Your Skinny Arms To Grow" 4.8M with 217k saves; shoulders 1.5M), then mostly 5 to 35k. A big hit early, then decay.
- "Part 2/3" posts get a fraction of Part 1's views.
- Titles are physique promises. **For EARND, reframe as progression and tracking** ("How to force your bench to go up", with numbers labelled example).

## C. Flat-vector tip animation (@jabmaxapp), the closest analogue to EARND
- A prelaunch iOS app with a "Coming soon" bio and a **waitlist link** (jabmax.app/waitlist). 93k followers in about 6 weeks, from 22 videos.
- Every video is about 61 s. Bold condensed headline ("YOU'RE TOO TENSE"), stick-figure boxers, red vs green comparisons.
- Hooks: a famous fighter's story ("Nikolai Isaev's drill" 3.9M, "Mike Tyson's peek-a-boo" 360k) or one fix ("You're not out of shape. You're holding your breath").
- EARND fit: high. Run it as strength-history stories and one-fix tips, with a waitlist CTA.

## D. Minimal relatable loops (@neuronvisuals)
- Dark minimal vector scenes, a single light source, small labels ("ME" / "MY WIFE"). **Most are 12 to 16 s**, built to loop.
- Couples, friends and siblings, plus mindset lines. Captions: "Send this to ...". Median 546k from a 74k account, which makes it share-driven.
- EARND fit: medium. Gym-relatable versions ("ME / MY GYM BRO", "ME / MY NOTES APP").

## E. 3D comedic character skits (@try.lockin, @suppose.there.was.time, @dropship ad)
- Pixar-like or translucent "x-ray skeleton" 3D characters in situations, with a one-word label ("BRO", "BABE"). 45 to 110 s.
- try.lockin (a self-improvement app): gym-bro skeleton skits, 4 to 5M views each.
- @dropship (5.1M-follower brand) used a couple skit as the hook for a product pitch: 7.3M. **Hook-pivot** works for an app.
- EARND fit: high for reach. Gym skits about forgetting weights or guessing the plan, and a hook-pivot to EARND on the final beat.
- The character must be original, not a copy of Bob or the lockin skeletons.

## Across all of these
- All animated, no faces, so the accounts are cheap to scale per format.
- Prelaunch apps (Jabmax) grow with a waitlist link. EARND currently has none.
- Megahits are rare (1 to 2 per account). Medians are what matter for planning.
