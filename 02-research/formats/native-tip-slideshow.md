# Format: native-text tip slideshow (@gymbratzzzz / @mayaviefit)

Added 2026-11-01. The founder chose these two accounts as the model for **@ezyxbt**. Data: Apify scrape on 2026-11-01, top 30 posts by popularity from each account.

## The numbers
- **@gymbratzzzz**: 73.7K followers, 382 posts. Bio: "real gym tips for real results · track workouts + food ↓". Every one of the top 30 posts is a photo slideshow. Views range from 245K to 4.6M, saves from 6K to 300K. The top posts are core, glute and back routines.
- **@mayaviefit**: 131.7K followers, 154 posts. About 80% of the top 30 are slideshows: high-protein recipes and glute/back routines, with views from 211K to 11M.
- Save rates are very high (often 5–8% of views). The posts work as reference material people save for later.

## Mechanics (from their top slides)
1. **9:16 full-screen (1080×1920)**, not 3:4.
2. **Slide 1 = the creator in the gym** (mirror selfie). The hook is in TikTok's native text style: white text, sometimes in a white pill. Tone is first person and casual ("My top core exercises that ACTUALLY build my abs", "how I would grow my glutes in 2026 (if I had to start over)"). A small muscle diagram sits on the photo.
3. **Slides 2–N = one exercise per slide.** Each has a **red-highlighted anatomy diagram** (the target muscle in red, everything else grey), the exercise name in a **white pill label**, and 1–3 lines of plain, personal small text ("great stretch, just DON'T rush through reps", "(my fav)"). Sometimes it's a 2×2 grid of real gym photos instead.
4. **A summary slide** with the full split or routine as a list over a darkened gym photo.
5. **Last slide = "REAL TIP (don't skip this)" / "Bonus tip"**: a hand holding a phone with a workout tracker open. The text reads "remember to track all your lifts. progressive overload is key." **This is the app plug, placed at the very end as a tip, not as a sales slide.** (They don't disclose it, e.g. "the app is called Soma btw"; we always disclose.)
6. Captions are hashtags only. Little or no branding on the slides.

## Why it works
- It looks like a real person's notes, not a brand's design: native text, real photos, no template.
- One exercise per slide with a clear diagram makes it easy to save.
- The app comes last, after the value is delivered, framed as advice ("track your lifts").

## Adapting it for @ezyxbt (our rules still apply)
- **Format:** 1080×1920, a founder photo on the cover (face-forward, clothed, from the closed library), and native-style white text and pills (Inter Bold; TikTok Sans couldn't be downloaded).
- **Diagrams:** our own generated anatomy renders (`01-app/brand/anatomy/`), not their stock illustrations. Posts carry TikTok's AI-generated label.
- **Hooks:** first person about what he *does*, never what it *built*. Banned: "exercises that ACTUALLY build my chest". Allowed: "the 4 chest exercises I actually do" or "how I'd set up my week if I started over". No physique promises, and photos are never framed as results (the library is closed and he has since lost weight).
- **App slide:** last, before the CTA. It's a "bonus tip": track every working set, and here's what I use. **Disclosed**: "i'm building it. it's called EARND." Real screenshot only. A real photo of his hand holding the phone with EARND open would match the format best (no body needed), so ask the founder for one.
- **Standing-rule deviation:** CLAUDE.md puts the app on slide 3–4 by default. This format puts it last. That needs the founder's OK; it's flagged at gate 3.
- Sets × reps plain (2 × 4–8); any weights labelled "example".
