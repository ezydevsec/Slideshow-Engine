# Format: anime coach explainer (+ character voice reference)

Added 2026-10-28 from two TikToks the founder supplied. Status: **not tested**. No set written yet.

## Source 1: format reference

- Link: https://www.tiktok.com/@h.a.m.e.e.d.k.h.a.n1/video/7688714096295906591
- Medium: **video** (106 s, 9:16), not photo mode. A narrated voiceover over a sequence of still illustrations with small cuts.
- Topic: "How To Force Your Skinny Arms To Grow" (brachialis, hammer curls, 3x12).

### Visual system
- Pure black background throughout.
- A **persistent title bar** at the top: white rounded box with black text, the same on every frame ("How To Force Your Skinny Arms To Grow").
- One recurring **original character**: grayscale manga-style lifter, messy hair, shirtless, **purple shorts** as the one colour accent. The same character appears in every beat, so viewers recognise it.
- Anatomy beats: a white line-art arm with the target muscle filled in **one flat colour** (blue, then red and green), plus small labels ("Width", "Height", "Depth").
- Exercise beats: the character performs the move, with simple red arrows for the grip and direction and a small grey label ("Hammer Curls").
- Text beats: a black screen with a single short line of small white text ("need to do...", "it's because you've been", "for you") as a pacing beat.
- Prescription beat: an icon plus "3 x 12 Reps".

### Script structure (timestamps from the voiceover)
1. 0–7 s **Pain hook, first person**: "If you're like me and you've struggled with skinny arms your entire life... chopsticks."
2. 7–18 s **Framework**: "three parts: width, height and depth. Train all three properly and..."
3. 18–33 s **Diagnosis**: "if your arms look okay from the side but small when you turn around, it's because you've been ignoring arm width."
4. 33–48 s **Insider reveal**: "barely anyone knows the muscle responsible... the brachialis."
5. 48–82 s **How to**: hammer curls, grip cue, slow lowering, "don't just drop the weight".
6. 82–97 s **Prescription and progression**: 3x12 close to failure; "if not burning, go heavier or slower".

### Why it works
- Specific body-part pain plus a named insult ("chopsticks") makes the hook easy to relate to.
- A simple framework (3 parts) promises a complete answer, so people watch to the end.
- The "barely anyone knows" reveal creates curiosity mid-video.
- One consistent character, one accent colour and one title bar make it instantly recognisable as a series.

## Source 2: character and voice reference ("Baki")

- Link: https://www.tiktok.com/@kvxgo/video/7693241941193985293
- Medium: video (33 s). An edit of **copyrighted Baki the Grappler anime footage**, with a voiceover and one-word captions.
- Script (full):
  > What if tomorrow, someone stronger than you decided your comfort was over? / What if your safety disappeared? / What if the only thing between you and destruction was your body? / Would you still train casually? / Or would you train like your life depends on it?
- Voice: deep, slow, low-register male narrator. Short rhetorical questions with pauses between them. The tone is serious, almost threatening, motivational.
- Visuals: high-contrast black and white anime, exaggerated muscle definition, hard shadows. **One word of the voiceover shown at a time**, centred in small white text.

## What we can and can't take

| Take | Don't take |
|---|---|
| Black canvas, persistent title bar, one recurring original character, one accent colour | Baki's likeness, name, footage, or a cloned voice of any real actor |
| Gritty, high-contrast manga art style (style isn't owned) | "Force your arms to grow" / "no choice but to grow" (physique promise, banned by standing rules) |
| Rhetorical-question cadence, deep slow narrator (an original Higgsfield voice) | "If you're like me and I've struggled..." from a fictional character (invented experience) |
| Pain hook → framework → reveal → how-to → prescription structure | Rep schemes without the "example" label |

## How it maps to our engine

- Our pipeline renders **photo-mode slideshows**. This format can run as a slideshow: one beat per slide, the title bar on every slide, 6–9 slides, under 25 words each. A narrated video version through Higgsfield (video plus an original voice) is a possible second test, not the default.
- Reframe the payoff toward tracking and having a plan: "train width, height and depth, and **track each one** so you can see when you've improved." The app slide (3 or 4) shows the real EARND screenshot of the logged lift. No generated UI.
- Every numerical example on a slide carries the "example" label.
